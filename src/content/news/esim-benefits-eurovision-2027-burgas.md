If you're flying in for Eurovision 2027, your phone is doing more work than usual: digital tickets at the Arena gates, hotel bookings, Maxim/TaxiMe for rides, Moovit for buses, translating menus, and posting from the EuroClub. None of that works without a reliable data connection - and that's exactly where most first-time visitors to Bulgaria trip up.

## The problem with the "old" ways of getting online

**Roaming with your home SIM** is the easiest option and, for most non-EU visitors, the most expensive one. Unless your carrier has a specific Bulgaria/EU add-on, per-MB roaming rates can burn through a data plan in a day.

**Buying a physical SIM at the airport** feels like the safe backup plan, but it's the one locals actively warn against. Airport SIM counters in Bulgaria are notorious for overcharging tourists compared to the same SIM bought in the city - and during Eurovision week you'll also be queuing for it right when you're most tired and just want to get to your hotel.

## Why an eSIM solves both problems

An eSIM is a digital SIM you install before you even leave home - no counter, no queue, no physical card to lose.

- **You land already connected.** Activate it on the plane or at the gate, and your maps, taxi app, and hotel confirmation are working the second you step off the jet bridge.
- **Your home number stays free.** Most phones support dual SIM, so you keep your regular number for calls and texts while the eSIM handles data - no need to give up WhatsApp verification or 2FA codes tied to your usual line.
- **Pricing is transparent up front.** You see the exact plan and price in the app before you buy, instead of finding out what "roaming" cost you when the bill arrives.
- **It covers the whole trip, not just Burgas.** If you're routing through Sofia, Istanbul, or another EU country on the way, a regional eSIM plan covers the whole itinerary on one purchase.
